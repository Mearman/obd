# Mercedes-Benz compact cars: A-Class, B-Class, CLA, GLA and Citan

The compact cars group by diagnostic layout, not by badge. The MFA platform (W176 A-Class, W246 B-Class, C117 CLA, X156 GLA) shares one UDS address table and has the `mercedes-mfa` profile. The W169 A-Class and W245 B-Class have only an engine address in the open sources and the `mercedes-w169-w245` profile, which holds the engine and three further units named only by the identifiers that answered a sweep of one A 150. The W168 A-Class has no open source and no profile ([issue 89](https://github.com/Mearman/obd/issues/89)). The Citan W415 uses the Renault Alliance address plan and belongs with `renault-dacia-core` ([issue 90](https://github.com/Mearman/obd/issues/90), sources in [europe-other.md](europe-other.md)). This note extends [mercedes-benz.md](mercedes-benz.md), which covers the gateway, CAN-B and the KWP2000 conventions, and does not repeat it.

## MFA: W176, W246, C117 and X156

**Bus and protocol.** The OBD socket's CAN at 500 kbit/s with 11-bit identifiers: a W246 B 180 CDI repository states ISO 15765-4 CAN, 11-bit, 500 kbit/s, with the engine at 0x7E0 and 0x7E8 answering UDS service 22 in the default session without security access ([w246-b180cdi-obd README](https://github.com/vicoMaz/w246-b180cdi-obd)). On a W176, Memostats-V2 records the MBito app's scan probing each endpoint with `10 03` and two units answering `50 03 00 14 00 C8` (P2 20 ms, P2* 2000 ms), then reading `22 F1 00` (variant), `22 F1 8C` (serial), `22 F1 11` (hardware number) and `22 F1 21` (software number), with raw frames captured from three units ([PROTOCOL.md, section 11](https://github.com/Memo1309/Memostats-V2/blob/main/memostats-v2/docs/PROTOCOL.md), [W176-ECUS.md](https://github.com/Memo1309/Memostats-V2/blob/main/memostats-v2/docs/W176-ECUS.md)). Fault codes are read with `19 02 0D` and counted with `19 01 0D` on the engine (user-reported in the same file). The profile therefore uses UDS and opens session 0x03, which the guard permits.

**Addresses.** The W176-ECUS.md table lists 70 request and response pairs from the MBito app's W176 catalogue, which its author exported from the app's database; 23 are on one W176's MBito record and were reported answering the official scan, and the other 47 are catalogue entries that may be optional equipment or other markets' units (sirens, SDARS, Russian e-call). Most units reply on 0x4xx identifiers (instrument cluster 0x60A to 0x481, ESP 0x632 to 0x486, airbags 0x64A to 0x489), not on the request plus 8. The catalogue types some units `OLD_ECU` or `OLD_ECU_MANSPEC` (the DCT transmission, the HVAC246 climate control, the FSCM212); Memostats-V2 infers that these take KWP2000-style identification (`1A 86`, `1A 87`, `1A 9D`), and that is unconfirmed, so the profile keeps them on UDS.

**Corroboration from OBDb.** OBDb's per-model test captures (CC-BY-SA-4.0; addresses only are used) show 0x7E0 answering service 22 with replies on 0x7E8 on a 2014 A-Class ([A-180 2014](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-A-180/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2014/command_support.yaml)) and a 2015 GLA ([GLA 2015](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-GLA-Class/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2015/command_support.yaml)), 0x7E1 answering service 22 with replies on 0x7E9 on the same cars, 0x7E3 answering OBD mode 01 on the 2014 A-Class, and 0x7E5 answering service 22 on a 2017 A-Class ([A-180 2017](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-A-180/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2017/command_support.yaml)) and a 2018 CLA ([CLA 2018](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-CLA-Class/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2018/command_support.yaml)). Those request identifiers match the catalogue's engine (MED40), DCT, FSCM_EC and CEPC. A unit on 0x7E2 with replies on 0x7EA answers wheel-speed and acceleration identifiers on the 2017 A-Class and a 2018 CLA 200 ([CLA-200 2018](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-CLA-200/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2018/command_support.yaml)); the MBito catalogue has no 0x7E2 entry, so its identity is unknown. The model years were mapped to generations with OBDb's own generations file ([A-180 generations](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-A-180/blob/main/generations.yaml)); a 2018 A-Class could be a W176 or a W177, so OBDb's A-200 2018 capture is not used.

**Reach across the platform.** The catalogue is a W176 one. Its units carry other chassis numbers in their names (EPS246, HVAC246, FCW246, PARK117, also PTS246), which shows parts shared with the W246 and C117, and the profile is applied to all four MFA cars on that basis. For the W246, C117 and X156 this is an inference, apart from the engine, transmission and the 0x7E2 and 0x7E5 units that OBDb and w246-b180cdi-obd show on those cars. The MFA2 cars (W177, W247, C118, H247) are not covered: OBDb's 2020 CLA 200 capture answers the legislated requests on 29-bit identifiers (`DB33`), so MFA2 needs its own research.

**Adapters.** w246-b180cdi-obd reports that its ELM327 clone only passed replies on 0x7E8 to 0x7EF, so units replying on 0x4xx were not reachable with it, and that the clone stopped listening after `7F xx 78` (response pending) ([elm327-notes.md](https://github.com/vicoMaz/w246-b180cdi-obd/blob/main/docs/elm327-notes.md)). An adapter that honours `AT CRA` for any identifier is needed for the body units.

**Live values.** w246-b180cdi-obd lists engine data identifiers for the OM607 diesel (MIT licence). They belong to one engine, not the platform, so they are not in the profile; live values are [issue 6](https://github.com/Mearman/obd/issues/6).

## W169 A-Class and W245 B-Class

**Engine.** OBDb's 2011 A 180 capture has 0x7E0 answering service 22 for identifiers 0163, 0168 and 016F with multi-frame replies on 0x7E8, the reply starting `62 01 63` ([7E0.7E8.220163.yaml](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-A-180/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2011/commands/7E0.7E8.220163.yaml), [A-180 2011](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-A-180/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2011/command_support.yaml)). OBDb names those identifiers boost, exhaust back pressure and particulate filter pressure, and fuel temperature, so that car is a diesel. Its 2009 capture has 0x7E0 answering OBD mode 01 only ([A-180 2009](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-A-180/blob/main/tests/test_cases/2009/command_support.yaml)). OBDb's own generations file places 2004 to 2012 cars in the W169 ([A-180 generations](https://github.com/OBDb/Mercedes-Benz-A-180/blob/main/generations.yaml)). A petrol engine such as the M266 has a different controller, so the engine row is a lead for it, not a fact.

**Protocol.** Xplatforms/mercedes-hacking-docs chapter 3 lists the wake-up frame ID 0x001C carrying `02 10 92 00 00 00 00 00` for the W169 and the W245, the same as for the W211 ([chapter 3](https://github.com/Xplatforms/mercedes-hacking-docs/blob/master/Chapter%203%20Connecting%20to%20the%20Vehicle.md)). `10 92` is the KWP2000 session start the W211 profile uses, so the profile is KWP2000 with session 0x92. The frame is a broadcast that wakes interior units, not a request to the engine, the table is crowd-sourced, and the chapter's text is unfinished in places (its software and sending sections are placeholders), so the session on the engine is unverified. KWP2000 also defines service 22, which is consistent with the OBDb capture. The bit rate is the 500 kbit/s that [mercedes-benz.md](mercedes-benz.md) gives for CAN-C; OBDb's captures show 11-bit identifiers but not the rate.

**Left out.** No open source gives a W169 or W245 address for the transmission, ESP, airbags, cluster or any interior unit. OBDb's 2011 capture lists 0x7E1, 0x7E2 and 0x7E5 requests as unsupported on that car. [Issue 88](https://github.com/Mearman/obd/issues/88) holds the revisit trigger.

## W168 A-Class

No source opened gives the W168's diagnostic socket line assignments, its unit addresses or initialisation, or a captured exchange. The youcanic list of 38-pin socket models does not include the A-Class ([youcanic](https://www.youcanic.com/mercedes-benz-models-with-38-pin-port/)), and OBDb's A-Class captures start with the W169. There is no profile; [issue 89](https://github.com/Mearman/obd/issues/89) holds the revisit trigger.

## Sources and limits

- Memostats-V2 `W176-ECUS.md` and `PROTOCOL.md` (no licence stated; addresses and protocol facts only): the generated tables were read in full. The underlying JSON catalogue (`memostats-w176-ecu-db-2026-09-29.json`) and the raw capture evidence file were not opened. The catalogue was exported from a commercial app's database; the on-car rows are the author's reports, and only three units have raw frames.
- OBDb A-180, GLA-Class, CLA-Class and CLA-200 test captures (CC-BY-SA-4.0): `command_support.yaml` for each year was read in full; the 2011 A-180 `7E0.7E8.220163.yaml` capture was read in full; its blob page could not be archived (the save returned an error on every attempt), so the raw file's snapshot is cited.
- Xplatforms/mercedes-hacking-docs chapter 3: read in full; the chapter itself is incomplete, as noted above.
- w246-b180cdi-obd (MIT): README and `docs/elm327-notes.md` read in full; the drive logs were not.
